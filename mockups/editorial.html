№ 01 on infrastructure work,  keyboards,  and the  quiet years
"I came up through a bootcamp, then spent three years on infrastructure that nobody throws a launch party for."
A working software engineer · Brooklyn, NY

Gary Rivera

I'm a software engineer in New York. I came up through a bootcamp, spent three years on the backend at a fintech that got acquired, and in my off-hours I build mechanical keyboards.

By the desk · Filed May 2026 · ↗ resume
01   Work2021 — present

At Orchard from 2022 to 2024, I did the unglamorous part of an acquisition. We bridged Extra's customer pipeline into Orchard's mortgage product after the buy, which meant rewriting the customer-state machine and shipping it without dropping anyone mid-funnel. It is the work I'm proudest of and the work that is hardest to describe at a party.

Before that, two years at Extra in the city: the kind of cloud-infrastructure work nobody throws a launch party for. We moved off DigitalOcean onto AWS with zero downtime, designed the permissions framework all of our internal APIs now talk through, and got the codebase onto TypeScript with shared backend types. I was promoted to senior in the middle of all of it, which is a sentence that means more in retrospect than it did at the time.

Before that, I went to Rithm School in New York, did a short internship at Numbers API, freelanced at KnowledgeCapital for a couple of weeks, and got hired at Extra in September of 2021. That sequence — bootcamp, internship, freelance, full-time — happened in about ninety days, which is the kind of thing I only mention now because the years between then and now have stretched out.
